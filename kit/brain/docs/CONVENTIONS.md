# Detailed memory conventions

Read this when you create or reorganize a mission, a source, an action, a decision, a person page, a note or a handoff. The definition of a project and the "what goes where" map are in [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md) ("Where information goes"), and nowhere else; this document gives the formats. Daily habits are in [`WORKFLOWS.md`](WORKFLOWS.md).

## Folders and navigation

- Only `<client>.md` sits at the root of a client folder; a mission is never called `index.md`. A project's subfolders are created when needed: for example `projects/internal/setup/notes/` and `projects/internal/setup/journal/` only exist from their first entry. The axes of `projects/` do not mirror the buckets of `../repos/`; the project page carries the pointer to its repositories: their folder names and, on its `Repo:` line, `uv run tools/repos/client-repos.py list --project <project>`, never a local path or a link into `repos/` (each entry of `.data/client-repos.json` names its project).
- A project page is a short index: goal, status, brief state, links and the timeline of the current month. Do not copy the repo's implementation into the brain.
- **Four forms, one test.** A **source** is the dated trace of what someone said or sent (meeting minutes, mail, Teams, provenance note from a third party), kept in `sources/YYYY-MM/` and never rewritten. A **note** (`notes/`) is the brain owner's dated working note (idea, analysis, preparation), editable. A **workstream** is a mission reference document kept up to date (technical context, architecture, repo guide, overview, living state of a recurring topic), named by topic and never dated. The **timeline** gives one dated line per event on the project page (current month), and past months go to `journal/`.
- A note is a flat file `<project>/notes/YYYY-MM-DD-<topic>.md` that starts with its title and a link to the project page (brain convention). There is no `notes/` or `topics/` at the root of the brain.

## Sources, state and journal

- **Language of the structure.** The tools write only the English structure of the templates (`## Open`, `created:`, `#group/`, `## Accepted`, `## Current state`, `Previous months:`…), whatever the language of the content; the registers are read only in English. A brain written with the earlier French structure is converted once with `uv run tools/brain/migrate-english.py` (dry run by default, `--write` to apply, `--mapping` for its table); its instruction files (`AGENTS.md`, `README.md`, `docs/`) are translated by hand.
- A substantial source = `sources/YYYY-MM/YYYY-MM-DD-<cr|mail|teams|note>-<slug>.md`, dated from the start of the meeting or thread; `cr` means meeting minutes (from the French *compte rendu*). A multi-file export keeps its folder under the month, with `index.md`. Two sources with the same name and date take `-2`, then `-3`; never overwrite. A source stays stable: correct it with a dated addendum.
- `sources/YYYY-MM/index.md` lists each entry of the month once: date, type, subject (15 words at most) and link. The project page points to this index. The timeline repeats a source only if it marks a milestone or a change of state; a meeting that does neither gets its index line only. A source without a clear project goes into `inbox/inbox.md` with the question to settle, with no invented filing.
- The templates are all in `kit/brain/templates/` (`_CLIENT-`, `_MISSION-`, `_ACTIONS-`, `_DECISIONS-`, `_SOURCES-INDEX-`, `_PROJECT-`, `_HANDOFF-`, `_NOTE-`, `_PERSON-`, `_CR-TEMPLATE.md`); their links are valid for the destination given at the top of each template. In Obsidian, insert them with "Templates: Insert template". Remove the fictional examples and, in meeting minutes, the empty optional sections.
- `state/actions.md` has `Open`, `Done`, `Cancelled`. The action line is `- [state] **<ID>** — <owner> — <title>`, followed if needed by the Tasks metadata: priority (`⏫`, `🔼`, `🔽`), creation date `➕ YYYY-MM-DD` (set once, at creation), precise due date `📅 YYYY-MM-DD`, chosen work date `⏳ YYYY-MM-DD`. This is what the board displays. A group of tasks is written with an optional tag `#group/<slug>` (one per action, at the end of the title, just before these emojis; slug in lowercase letters, digits and hyphens), set by `action.py --group` and not by hand. A precise `📅` date is authoritative: do not duplicate it in a sub-bullet, because editing in Tasks does not sync sub-bullets. In sub-bullets indented by four spaces: `due: <nuance or —>` (for wordings that cannot be converted into a precise date), `source: [link]`, `created: YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` and `updated: YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` (local time, to the minute; `updated` changes only when the action really changes, and an action brought back to its last Git synchronization point gets back its previous `updated`), then one dated bullet per change of state, owner, due date or blocker (`DD/MM: …`, 35 words at most; a mention that changes none of them adds no bullet); a closed action also has `done: <dated evidence>`, a cancelled one `cancelled: <date> by <person> — [source](…)`. Creating, changing, closing, completing the history of or deleting an action is done with `uv run tools/brain/action.py` (see [its guide](../../../tools/brain/action.md)), which writes the metadata and files the action in its section. States: `[ ]` to do, `[/]` in progress, `[w]` waiting, `[x]` done, `[-]` cancelled. Closing an action means `[x]` or `[-]` with `✅ YYYY-MM-DD` or `❌ YYYY-MM-DD`, dated at the evidence. An action ticked from the board stays in place until `uv run tools/brain/action.py sweep --write`: `[x]` goes to `Done`, `[-]` to `Cancelled`, a reopened action to `Open`. A cancellation does not erase the ID. The `SET` prefix is reserved for `projects/internal/setup/`; each new client or project gets a two- to five-letter prefix validated by the brain owner. Look for the highest ID in the whole folder, sources and archives included, before assigning the next one; never reuse an ID.
- **Actions archive (brain convention).** A closed action whose closing date (`✅` or `❌ YYYY-MM-DD`) is more than 30 days old leaves `Done` or `Cancelled`, with all its sub-bullets, for `state/actions-archive-YYYY.md` (YYYY = year of closing), next to the register. The file keeps the `## Done` and `## Cancelled` sections and opens with `# Actions archive YYYY — <project>` then `> Closed actions of <project> archived for YYYY; the live register is [actions.md](actions.md). IDs stay reserved.` A closed action without a closing date stays in the register. The highest ID is searched archives included. Only `state/actions.md` feeds the board, `tools/brain/action.py board`, the web interface and the doctor's count; the history of `tasks.md` also reads the archives. The agent archives without asking, as soon as the doctor reports it or it writes in the project in a new month: `uv run tools/brain/action.py archive` (dry run, repeated in its summary), then `archive --write` ([guide](../../../tools/brain/action.md)).
- An action is written once, in its register; no view copies it. Do not infer that an action is finished from the existence of a draft or a proposal.
- **Subtasks.** An action can hold up to 10 steps, written right under its line as `    - [ ] <text>` or `    - [x] <text>` (15 words at most, no ID, owner or date: they inherit the action's), with `uv run tools/brain/action.py subtask add|toggle|edit|remove|move`, or ticked by hand in Obsidian. Ticking one refreshes `updated:` but adds no history entry. A step with its own owner or due date is an action of the same group (`--group`), not a subtask. An action can be closed with subtasks still open: the tool says how many.
- **When an action is created** (edge cases of the four tests of [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md#source-of-truth)). Test 2: a commitment the brain owner shares (`A / B`, `A, then B`) counts as carried by the brain owner; the action's owner is whoever carries it. Test 3: a promise whose stated time has passed when the agent writes is proposed with a `?`. Test 4: the covering action gets one dated history entry, and its due date or owner changes only if the commitment changes them; an action the brain owner asks for still passes this test. Due date: write the one that was stated (`today` is a date), otherwise create the action without one and ask the brain owner for it at validation.
- **Validation table.** `cr-reunion` and `veille-messages` show it before writing to a register, one row per commitment that is new in the source: `#`, commitment (15 words at most), carrier, due date or `none`, proposal (`create`, `update <ID>`, `subtask <ID>` or `not tracked`, with a `?` when unsure) and reason (for `create`, the branch of test 2; for `update`, what changes; for `subtask`, the step added to that action; for `not tracked`, the test that fails). They apply only the rows the brain owner validates; without an answer, nothing. A source that reports on an action already in the register updates it without validation. `inbox` validates through its routing table.
- `state/decisions.md` distinguishes `Proposed`, `Accepted`, `Rejected / superseded`, with source and acceptance date. A decision heard in a meeting stays a proposal until the brain owner has filed it. An accepted decision is not rewritten: add a new decision and mark the old one superseded; rejected ones keep a short trace. Each decision is a block `### Title (YYYY-MM-DD, status: proposed|accepted|rejected|superseded)` followed by the bullets `**Context:**`, `**Decision:**`, `**Consequences:**`, `**Source:**` (70 words at most for the title and the first three together; `Context` only when the source does not give it), then `**Accepted:**`, `**Rejected:**` or `**Superseded by:**`; in Accepted and in Rejected / superseded, the most recent comes first. These blocks are written with `uv run tools/brain/decision.py` (`propose`, `accept`, `reject`, `supersede`; [guide](../../../tools/brain/decision.md)). The project page points to this register and keeps no list of decisions.
- `state/glossary.md` (optional) lists the project's terms, acronyms and frequent transcription errors, to recognize names and topics in the minutes; `cr-reunion` reads it if it exists.
- **Timeline and journal (brain convention).** The `## Timeline` of a project page keeps only the entries of the current month. As soon as an agent writes in the project in a new month, or the `brain-clean` doctor reports it, it moves the earlier entries without asking, unchanged and in the same order (most recent first), into `<project>/journal/YYYY-MM.md`, one file per month: title, back link to the project page, then `> Journal archive of <project> for <Month YYYY>: entries moved from the project page on YYYY-MM-DD. The current month stays on the page.` The relative links of the moved entries are corrected for the depth of one folder. The page keeps under `## Timeline` a line `Previous months: [2026-09](journal/2026-09.md) · …`. The journal keeps the milestones, without redoing the summary of each source. An event of an already archived month, handled late, is inserted at its date in the `journal/YYYY-MM.md` of its month, not on the page. `uv run tools/brain/timeline.py` writes the entries and does these moves (`add`, `roll --write`; [guide](../../../tools/brain/timeline.md)).

## Tasks board and inbox

- `tasks.md` is the single focus page, with `cssclasses: tasks-dashboard`. "Today" shows the actions that are not waiting, scheduled up to today or overdue; "In progress" the started actions; "Up next" the first 8 available actions by priority then due date; "Follow up" every `[w]`; a collapsed section shows the due dates of the next 7 days. The per-project list and the kanban show every owner until the starter has been personalized. To filter the personal views, fill in `.data/doctor.json`, then add a Tasks filter on the owner if needed. The 15 most recent dated closures are collapsed, archives included. An "Other projects" callout catches new registers; add a dedicated callout for a project you follow. No task is written in the page: the Tasks queries only read `projects/**/state/actions.md` of the single brain that carries this page, and `actions-archive-YYYY.md` for the history, without JavaScript.
- Add an action from Obsidian: click **New action**, at the top of `tasks.md` (Meta Bind installed and enabled). The button runs the template `kit/brain/templates/templater/new-action.md` through Meta Bind's [runTemplaterFile](https://www.moritzjung.dev/obsidian-meta-bind-plugin-docs/reference/buttonactions/runtemplaterfile/) action. The form offers the registers of every brain of the workspace (the brain name prefixes the project as soon as there are several; the default owner comes from the `.data/doctor.json` of the chosen brain) and asks for project, title, owner, state, priority and due date; an invalid date must be corrected, and Cancel stops the addition. It assigns the next ID (highest ID of the client folder, sources included) and writes into `Open`, with `source: owner, DD/MM`. The due date is written directly in the Tasks format `📅 YYYY-MM-DD`. Keyboard alternative: `Cmd+P` → search `new-action` → select **Templater: Insert**. Do not use the core Templates plugin, which would copy the code.
- Obsidian settings required: Tasks, Templater and Meta Bind; JavaScript disabled in Tasks and Meta Bind; status `/` named In Progress, of type `IN_PROGRESS`; custom status `w` named `Waiting`, next status `x`, type `ON_HOLD`; status `-` named Cancelled, type `CANCELLED`; CSS snippet `tasks-board` enabled. The `tasks.md` queries rely on the type `ON_HOLD` (Tasks 7 or later), not on the name of the status. A brain whose `tasks.md` still filters on the earlier status name (`status.name includes attente`) keeps the former setting of `w` (that name, type `TODO`) until the status is switched to `ON_HOLD` in Obsidian; `migrate-english.py --write` then converts those queries. The starter does not manage `.obsidian/` (ignored, local to each machine): set these by hand once per machine, following the guide `kit/obsidian/README.md` (the CSS snippet is `kit/obsidian/tasks-board.css`, to copy into `.obsidian/snippets/`). Open the workspace root as the vault so that the paths of the button and the form work.
- Outside Obsidian, from the workspace root: `uv run tools/brain/action.py board` shows the same registers. `--write` generates `agents/tasks-board.md` for VS Code; `--write --watch` keeps it up to date, and the "Tasks board (live)" task of `.vscode/tasks.json` in the brain starts it when the brain is opened. The Todo Tree extension, configured in `.vscode/settings.json` of the brain, lists the open actions.
- The [local web interface](../../../tools/tasks-web/README.md) (`uv run tools/tasks-web/tasks-web.py`) has a sidebar with the Today, Actions, Board, Timeline, Notes, Search, Tools and Sync views, and the **New action** and **New note** buttons; it writes into the same registers and into project notes. Its Sync view saves and sends the changes of each brain after a confirmation (one card per brain, never Work). Prefer the cancelled state to deletion to keep the trace of an abandoned action.

## People, notes and voice

- A person page requires a first **and** last name: `people/first-last.md` (lowercase, no accents). Without a confirmed family name, cite the person in the mission and ask the question; do not create a page. Before creating one, look for aliases, variants and addresses in all the pages. YAML front matter: `name`, `organization`, `missions`, `aliases`, `role`, `email`, `updated`. Sections: `## Role and scope`, `## Expectations`, `## Positions taken`, `## Way of working`, `## Interaction log`, and optionally `## Mentoring / progression`.
- Content of a person page: professional role, scope, expectations, positions and way of working. No personal judgement, private life, health, opinions or phone number. Every addition cites a dated source or "owner, DD/MM"; never invent a role, date or decision.
- **Writing a lesson.** A lesson specific to one mission goes into that mission's `notes/`. A lesson reusable **beyond** one mission goes into `projects/internal/setup/notes/` (`YYYY-MM-DD-<topic>.md`) only after a second occurrence in another project **and** a dated incident of at least three lines. Write the observable symptom, the cause, the fix and the date, then point to the reference implementation. A perishable machine config also goes into `projects/internal/setup/notes/`, as a dated note.
- The Voice section of `me/about-me.md` is fed after a real example or a correction from the brain owner: a dated line under "Feed the voice", proposed before it is added, the substance rather than a full copy of a mail, a rule only if it holds across two sends; do not feed it with generalities.

## Links, handoffs and archives

- **Relative** Markdown links, with the `.md` extension; no wikilinks and no workspace-root links. After a move in a shell or with `git mv`, fix all the backlinks and run `uv run tools/brain/check-links.py` from the workspace root. VS Code can update the links when a file is moved in its explorer (`markdown.updateLinksOnFileMove.enabled: "always"`). No link from one brain to another brain (`check-links.py` reports it and fails, even if the target exists): a brain is a trust boundary. No link into `repos/` and no `repos/<group>/<name>` path either: write the repository's name in backticks, since the clone may be missing on another machine (rule and exceptions in [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md#where-information-goes)). No orphans.
- A useful handoff goes into `<project>/handoffs/`, named `YYYY-MM-DD-handoff-<slug>.md` and based on `kit/brain/templates/_HANDOFF-TEMPLATE.md`. Deliver it with a short launch message in a code block: mission, first action and the validations still needed.
- Archiving is an explicit gesture, requested by the brain owner (apart from the monthly `journal/` and `actions-archive-YYYY.md`, which follow their own rule). The `archive/` folder sits at the level of what it holds: a closed client mission moves whole into `projects/clients/<client>/missions/archive/<mission>/`, the client index staying in place; a closed internal project into `projects/internal/archive/<project>/`; a closed client, with all its missions, into `projects/clients/archive/<client>/`; an inactive person page into `people/archive/`. A project's notes, sources and journal are archived with it. Archive with `uv run tools/brain/brain-state.py archive <project>` (`clients/<client>` for a whole client; a dry run that shows what moves and the links it rewrites), then `--write`: it refuses while an action is still to do, in progress or waiting, moves the folder, rewrites the links into it and, one level deeper, those inside it that point out, and runs the link check; `unarchive` is the exact inverse. Archiving the last mission of a client does not archive the client. The earlier places (`projects/archive/<project>/`, `projects/clients/<client>/archive/<mission>/`) still count as archived; `archive` files them in the new ones. An archived project leaves every command, the web interface and the `tasks.md` dashboards; `brain-state.py brief <project> --archived` and `find <term> --archived` read it. A completed action does not mean the whole folder is archived.

## Ingestion and confidentiality

- Transcript minutes: follow the workspace's `cr-reunion` skill; the minutes are a Markdown source; transcript and audio stay at their original location, outside the workspace, and the minutes describe their provenance without a local path or file name. Mails and team messages: follow the `veille-messages` skill; the brain owner validates the sorting before anything is written. A minor message becomes a timeline line only if it changes a useful state.
- Each brain is a trust boundary declared in `.data/brain.json` (`scope`, `description`, `remote`, `email`, `allow`); content from another scope (personal, employer, a client of another brain…) goes into the brain whose `description` provides for it, never into this one, and the brain owner is asked when two brains fit. The names of clients of another brain (folders of its `projects/clients/`) do not appear in this brain, except those in `allow`; `uv run tools/machine/check-no-client.py --brain <brain> --staged` (or `--worktree`) checks this, and the pre-commit hook does it at every commit. `tools/brain/brain-state.py` merges the processing states of the brains without mixing their content; a skill's state is written in the `.data/` of the brain concerned. Never copy a secret, a password, a token or a single-use link: only note that access was provided.
- Memory additions cite their provenance. The dated timeline lines carry the model that wrote them, in parentheses, to keep this editorial provenance.

## Form

- Plain, skimmable Markdown. One physical line per item or paragraph; no artificial line breaks. Many small linked files beat one monolithic page.
- An unknown value stays `(to be completed)` or a question to the brain owner; never invent anything to fill in a template.
- **Lengths.** Words are counted between spaces, a link counting for its label. Refused by the tools: timeline entry 40 (`timeline.py add`), action history entry 35 (`action.py history`), decision 70 for title, context, decision and consequences together (`decision.py propose`), current-state block of a project page 150 in 6 bullets at most (`timeline.py state`, which replaces the block). Reported by the `brain-clean` doctor, which also counts what bypassed the tools: `Summary` of a project page 150, index subject 15, minutes over their budget (body of at most 50 words per minute of meeting up to 30 minutes, then 20 per further minute; 250 always allowed, 2,000 never exceeded; a `Key points` bullet 90).
